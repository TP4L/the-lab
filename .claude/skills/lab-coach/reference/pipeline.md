# The Pipeline

`Context → See → Read → State → Need → Solve → Predict → Organize → Reassess → Adapt`

The order is the discipline. Most bad decisions are not bad choices — they are
choices made from a stage that was skipped.

---

## Context

The standing conditions that were true before this rep started: score, clock,
field position, personnel on and off, matchup history, what the last three
possessions taught both sides.

Context sets the **price of a mistake**. The identical Read produces a different
solve at 2-goal-up-two-minutes than it does at even-early.

---

## See

Perception only. What is physically there — bodies, sticks, ball, space, the
support that exists and the support that does not.

Keep See separate from Read. Collapsing them is how you end up defending the
picture you expected instead of the one in front of you.

---

## Read

### Height × Time × Balance

**Height** — where the release point sits. High is loaded and ready; low means
the shot has to travel up before it can go anywhere, which is Time spent.

**Time** — the window before pressure arrives. Measured in what pressure has to
cover: steps, not seconds.

**Balance** — weight over base. Can force actually be transmitted, or is the
body still catching up to itself? A player who is balanced can change their mind;
a player who is not is committed.

Multiply them. Any zero is a zero.

### Orientation

Which way hips, eyes, and stick face relative to the target *and* the threat.
Orientation is what turns the other three factors into a direction. A player with
all three factors high but oriented away from goal is not a shot yet — they are a
shot one turn from now, and that turn is Time you can attack.

### Certainty

How confident the read is, honestly stated. Certainty does not change the
situation; it changes **how much you are allowed to commit to it**.

- High certainty → committing actions are legal (`Oh Slide Go`, `Hip Pocket Over`).
- Low certainty → only reversible actions are legal (`Cover Cover Sit`,
  `Go Stay Go`). Buy information before buying position.

Guessing with false confidence is a distinct error from reading wrong. See
Error Classification in `outer-layers.md`.

## State

**Defensive** — they hold the initiative; you are responding.
**Neutral** — neither side holds it; the next action decides who does.
**Offensive** — you hold the initiative; they are responding.

**Scramble is a condition layer, not a fourth state.** Scramble means structure
is broken and assignments are no longer where they were drawn. You can scramble
inside any of the three states. Always name both: "Offensive / Scramble",
"Defensive / Scramble".

The practical difference: a state tells you who is dictating. The Scramble layer
tells you that *the map is stale* — so Reassess frequency goes up and commitment
thresholds go up with it.

---

## Need

`Survive → Recover → Stabilize → Build → Pressure → Finish`

A ladder, climbed one rung at a time.

| Rung | You are trying to |
|---|---|
| **Survive** | Not lose it right now. Deny the immediate shot or the immediate turnover. |
| **Recover** | Get body, feet, and position back to legal — repay the debt the last action created. |
| **Stabilize** | Re-establish structure. Everyone knows who has whom again. |
| **Build** | Accumulate advantage. Shape, spacing, numbers. |
| **Pressure** | Force the opponent into a decision they do not want. |
| **Finish** | Convert the advantage. |

**Rung-skipping is the most common error in the whole model.** A player on
Survive who reaches for a Build action does not get Build — they get a worse
Survive. Name the rung before choosing the action, and only offer actions that
rung can legally hold.

Climbing is earned, not chosen. You move up when the previous rung is actually
satisfied, not when you would prefer to be higher.

---

## Solve

Four weights on every candidate action:

- **Risk** — how likely is this to fail?
- **Consequence** — if it fails, how bad? Risk and Consequence are independent;
  a high-risk / low-consequence action is often the correct one.
- **Cost** — what does it spend even when it *works*? Legs, position, Recovery
  Debt, a teammate's slot. Actions that succeed but cost more than they buy are
  how a rep is won and a possession lost.
- **Target** — what specifically is it aimed at? An action without a named target
  is effort, not a solve. Aim at the factor: "this takes Time", "this takes
  Balance."

The best solve is usually the one that zeroes the cheapest factor at the lowest
Cost — not the most aggressive one available.

---

## Predict

State what the opponent does in response. One beat, concretely.

Prediction is what makes Organize possible: you cannot cover a reaction you never
named. It also makes you accountable — a wrong prediction is diagnosable, a
vague one is not.

---

## Organize

Three jobs, and everyone on the field has exactly one of them right now:

- **Move** — the person acting on the ball or the space.
- **Cover** — the person picking up what Move vacates.
- **Recover** — the person repaying position, getting back into structure.

Organize fails when two people take the same job, or when nobody takes Recover.
The Recover job is the one that gets dropped, and it is the one that decides
whether the *next* rep starts in Scramble.

---

## Reassess

Re-run See → Read. Mandatory, not optional. You acted, so the situation is now a
different situation — including the factors you just changed.

Under the Scramble layer, Reassess fires more often and on a shorter fuse.

---

## Adapt

Two legal moves out of Reassess:

1. **Adjust the solve** — same Need rung, different action, because the Read
   changed or the Prediction missed.
2. **Step the Need** — the rung is satisfied, climb; or the rung was lost, drop.

Dropping a rung is not failure. Refusing to drop when the situation demands it
is — that is a Survive problem being answered with a Build action.
